\title{Subcube isolation in hypercubes is radius-covering:\\
perfect-code rigidity, a phase transition, and exponential additive gaps}
\author{Working draft}
\date{September 2026}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
Bre\v{s}ar and Rall asked whether the minimum number of vertices whose closed
neighborhoods destroy every copy of $Q_k$ in $Q_n$ always equals
$\gamma(Q_{n-k})$.  We identify the invariant exactly with a binary
radius-covering-array number.  This corrects the provenance of the smallest
counterexample, whose numerical value is established covering-array prior art,
and turns the graph question into a robust covering-code problem.  We prove a
sharp fixed-codimension transition: the parameter is constant in codimensions
at most three and grows logarithmically from codimension four onward.  A
perfect-code projection obstruction gives, for every binary Hamming length
$m=2^t-1$ with $t\ge3$,
\[
  \iota(Q_{m+k},Q_k)=\gamma(Q_m)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad k=1.
\]
We further prove the quantitative candidate bound
\[
 \iota(Q_{m+2},Q_2)-\gamma(Q_m)
 \ge
 \left\lceil
 \frac{3\gamma(Q_m)m(m-3)}{4(m+2)(m+1)^4}
 \right\rceil,
\]
so the additive failure is $\Omega(2^m/m^4)$ along the Hamming family.  The
coordinate-copy theorem, the graph-to-array bridge, the perfect-projection
metric core, and the quantitative algebra are accompanied by Lean
verification; the final incidence aggregation remains an explicit
end-to-end formalization gate.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Let $Q_n$ be the graph on $\{0,1\}^n$ with edges joining words at Hamming
distance one.  For graphs $G$ and $H$, the $H$-isolation number $\iota(G,H)$
is the minimum cardinality of a set $D\subseteq V(G)$ such that
$G-N[D]$ contains no copy of $H$.  Bre\v{s}ar and Rall asked whether
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BR}
  \iota(Q_n,Q_k)=\gamma(Q_{n-k})
\end{equation}
for every $0<k<n$.

The key observation is that the projected domination condition occurring in
this problem is not a new finite object: it is precisely a radius-covering
array, also called a generalized surjective code.  The translation makes the
proposed equality
\[
  \CAN_1(m,m+k,2)=\Kcov_2(m,1), \qquad m=n-k,
\]
where $\Kcov_q(m,r)$ is the minimum size of a $q$-ary radius-$r$ covering code.
The graph formulation therefore asks whether an optimal covering code always
admits arbitrarily many columns while remaining a covering code after every
prescribed puncturing.  It does not: perfect covering codes are rigid under
this robust-extension requirement, and near-perfect extensions must pay a
quantifiable overlap cost.

\section{Coordinate copies of hypercubes}

\begin{lemma}[Coordinate-copy lemma]\label{lem:coordinate-copy}
Every subgraph of $Q_n$ isomorphic to $Q_k$ is a coordinate $k$-subcube.
Equivalently, after translating one vertex, its embedding is obtained by
injecting the $k$ intrinsic coordinate directions into $k$ distinct ambient
coordinates.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Color every ambient edge by the unique coordinate that it flips.  Every
four-cycle in a hypercube alternates between two distinct colors, so opposite
edges have the same color.  In $Q_k$, all edges in one intrinsic direction are
connected through a sequence of opposite-edge relations in four-cycles;
therefore they have one common ambient color.  Edges in distinct intrinsic
directions meet at a vertex and hence receive distinct colors.  Starting from
one image vertex, every domain word is therefore mapped by independently
flipping the corresponding fixed ambient coordinates.
\end{proof}

\section{The radius-covering dictionary}

For $q\ge2$, an $N\times n$ $q$-ary array has radius-covering strength $m$ and
radius $r$ if every choice of $m$ columns has covering radius at most $r$ in
$[q]^m$.  Write $\CAN_r(m,n,q)$ for the least possible number of rows.

Define $\iota_r(Q_n,Q_k)$ to be the minimum size of $D\subseteq Q_n$ such that
$Q_n-N_r[D]$ contains no copy of $Q_k$.  Thus $\iota_1=\iota$.

\begin{theorem}[Exact dictionary]\label{thm:dictionary}
For $0\le m\le n$ and $r\ge0$,
\[
  \iota_r(Q_n,Q_{n-m})=\CAN_r(m,n,2).
\]
In particular,
\[
  \iota(Q_n,Q_k)=\CAN_1(n-k,n,2).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:coordinate-copy}, it suffices to hit every coordinate
$(n-m)$-face.  Such a face is specified by a set $S$ of $m$ fixed coordinates
and a pattern $a\in\{0,1\}^S$.  The minimum Hamming distance from a word $d$ to
that face is exactly $d_H(d|_S,a)$, because the free coordinates may be chosen
to agree with $d$.  Hence the radius-$r$ neighborhood of $D$ meets every such
face exactly when every $m$-column projection of the row array $D$ has covering
radius at most $r$.
\end{proof}

\section{A fixed-codimension phase transition}

Let
\[
  B_{m,r}=\sum_{j=0}^r {m\choose j}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Phase transition]\label{thm:phase}
Fix $m\ge1$ and $r\ge0$.  As $n\to\infty$,
\[
\CAN_r(m,n,2)=
\begin{cases}
1, & m\le r,\\
2, & r<m\le 2r+1,\\
\Theta_{m,r}(\log n), & m\ge 2r+2.
\end{cases}
\]
More explicitly, in the third regime every $M$-row array satisfies
\[
  n\le (m-1)2^{M-1},
\]
and a random construction gives
\[
 \CAN_r(m,n,2)
 \le 1+\left\lceil
 \frac{\log\!\left(2^m{n\choose m}\right)}
 {-\log\!\left(1-B_{m,r}/2^m\right)}
 \right\rceil.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If $m\le r$, one row covers every $m$-tuple.  If $r<m\le2r+1$, one row is
insufficient, while the two constant complementary rows cover every target
because $\min\{|a|,m-|a|\}\le\lfloor m/2\rfloor\le r$.

Now assume $m\ge2r+2$.  Complement columns independently so that the first row
is zero in every column.  This is an isometry in every projection and does not
change the radius-covering property.  The normalized columns therefore have
only $2^{M-1}$ possible types.  If one type occurred at least $m$ times,
selecting those columns would make every projected row constant, either
$0^m$ or $1^m$.  A target of weight $r+1$ is at distance at least $r+1$ from
both constants because $m-(r+1)\ge r+1$.  Hence each type occurs at most
$m-1$ times, giving $n\le(m-1)2^{M-1}$.

For the upper bound, choose $M$ independent uniform binary rows.  For a fixed
$m$-set of columns and a fixed target, the probability that no row lies within
radius $r$ is $(1-B_{m,r}/2^m)^M$.  A union bound over
$2^m{n\choose m}$ choices is less than one for the displayed value of $M$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:binary-phase}
For fixed $m$,
\[
\iota(Q_n,Q_{n-m})=
\begin{cases}
1,&m=1,\\
2,&m=2,3,\\
\Theta_m(\log n),&m\ge4.
\end{cases}
\]
Consequently, for every fixed $m\ge4$,
$\iota(Q_n,Q_{n-m})/\gamma(Q_m)\to\infty$.  In particular,
\[
 n>(m-1)2^{\gamma(Q_m)-1}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \iota(Q_n,Q_{n-m})>\gamma(Q_m).
\]
\end{corollary}

\section{Perfect projections and robust extension}

Let
\[
 \Vol_q(N,R)=\sum_{j=0}^R {N\choose j}(q-1)^j
\]
be the volume of a $q$-ary Hamming ball.

\begin{theorem}[Perfect-code extension obstruction]\label{thm:perfect}
Suppose a perfect $q$-ary radius-$r$ covering code of length $m$ exists, so
$\Kcov_q(m,r)\Vol_q(m,r)=q^m$.  Let $\ell\ge1$.  If
\[
 \Kcov_q(m,r)\Vol_q\!\left(m+\ell,
     r+\left\lfloor\ell/2\right\rfloor\right)>q^{m+\ell},
\]
then
\[
 \CAN_r(m,m+\ell,q)>\Kcov_q(m,r).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Assume an array with $\Kcov_q(m,r)$ rows exists.  Every $m$-column projection
is a radius-$r$ covering code of optimal perfect size.  Since the sum of the
sizes of its radius-$r$ balls equals the whole space, those balls partition the
space; thus each projection has minimum distance at least $2r+1$.

Take two full rows at distance $d$.  Delete $\ell$ coordinates containing as
many of their disagreements as possible.  If $d\le2r+\ell$, the remaining
$m$-projection has distance at most $2r$, contradiction.  Therefore the full
array has minimum distance at least $2r+1+\ell$.  Hamming balls of radius
$r+\lfloor\ell/2\rfloor$ around its rows are disjoint, and the Hamming packing
bound contradicts the displayed strict inequality.
\end{proof}

\section{Hamming families}

\begin{corollary}[Binary equality classification at Hamming codimensions]
\label{cor:binary-hamming}
Let $t\ge3$ and $m=2^t-1$.  For every $k\ge1$,
\[
  \iota(Q_{m+k},Q_k)=\gamma(Q_m)
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad k=1.
\]
In particular, $\iota(Q_{2^t+1},Q_2)>\gamma(Q_{2^t-1})$ for every $t\ge3$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
A binary Hamming code is a perfect radius-one code of length $m$.  Equality for
$k=1$ is the binary parity-extension theorem for radius-covering arrays.  For
$k=2$, Theorem~\ref{thm:perfect} applies because
\[
 \Vol_2(m+2,2)-4\Vol_2(m,1)=\frac{m(m-3)}2>0.
\]
Monotonicity in the number of columns gives strict inequality for every
$k\ge2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[q-ary Hamming family]\label{cor:qary-hamming}
Let $q$ be a prime power, $t\ge3$, and
$m=(q^t-1)/(q-1)$.  Then
\[
 \CAN_1(m,m+2,q)>\Kcov_q(m,1).
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The $q$-ary Hamming code is perfect, and
\[
 \Vol_q(m+2,2)-q^2\Vol_q(m,1)
 =\frac{(q-1)^2m(m+1-2q)}2>0.
\]
\end{proof}

\section{A quantitative Hamming-family gap}

The perfect-code obstruction proves an excess of at least one row.  The next
theorem shows that the absolute excess is in fact exponentially large.

\begin{theorem}[Quantitative gap]\label{thm:quantitative-gap}
Let $m=2^t-1$ with $t\ge3$, put
\[
 K=\gamma(Q_m)=\frac{2^m}{m+1},
 \qquad
 M=\iota(Q_{m+2},Q_2)=\CAN_1(m,m+2,2),
\]
and write $s=M-K$.  Then
\[
 s\ge
 \left\lceil
 \frac{3K m(m-3)}{4(m+2)(m+1)^4}
 \right\rceil.
\]
Consequently
\[
 \iota(Q_{m+2},Q_2)-\gamma(Q_m)
 =\Omega\!\left(\frac{2^m}{m^4}\right)
\]
along the binary Hamming lengths.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Take an optimal $M\times(m+2)$ binary radius-one covering array with distinct
rows.  For a deleted coordinate pair $D$ and $y\in Q_m$, let $c_D(y)$ be the
number of projected rows within distance one of $y$.  Every target is covered,
and double-counting incidences gives
\[
 \sum_y(c_D(y)-1)=(m+1)s.                                      \tag{1}
\]
For each $y$, there are $m+1$ possible projected centers and at most four full
lifts of each center, so $c_D(y)\le4(m+1)$.  Therefore
\[
 \sum_y {c_D(y)\choose2}
 \le 2(m+1)\sum_y(c_D(y)-1)
 =2(m+1)^2s.                                                    \tag{2}
\]
Let $e_D$ count unordered row pairs at projected distance at most two.  Such a
pair has at least two common radius-one neighbors, hence (2) implies
\[
 e_D\le(m+1)^2s.                                                \tag{3}
\]
Let $e$ count full row pairs at distance at most four.  Every such pair is
counted in at least six of the numbers $e_D$: for distance four, delete any two
of the four disagreements; the cases of smaller distance give at least as many
choices.  Summing (3) over all deleted pairs yields
\[
 6e\le {m+2\choose2}(m+1)^2s.                                  \tag{4}
\]

Now form a graph on the $M$ rows, joining pairs at distance at most four.  An
independent set is a length-$(m+2)$ binary code of minimum distance at least
five.  Radius-two sphere packing gives
\[
 \alpha(G)\le \frac{2^{m+2}}{V},
 \qquad
 V=1+(m+2)+{m+2\choose2}.
\]
Caro--Wei and Cauchy--Schwarz give
\[
 \alpha(G)\ge\frac{M^2}{M+2e},
\]
so
\[
 e\ge\frac12\left(\frac{M^2V}{2^{m+2}}-M\right).
\]
The right-hand side is increasing for $M\ge K$, and
\[
 \frac{KV}{2^{m+2}}-1=\frac{m(m-3)}{8(m+1)}.
\]
Thus
\[
 e\ge\frac{K m(m-3)}{16(m+1)}.                                \tag{5}
\]
Combining (4) and (5), and using
${m+2\choose2}=(m+2)(m+1)/2$, gives
\[
 s\ge\frac{3K m(m-3)}{4(m+2)(m+1)^4}.
\]
Since $s$ is integral, taking the ceiling completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The bound gives excess at least $1$ for $m=7$ and $15$, at least $1263$ for
$m=31$, and at least $374{,}653{,}301{,}052$ for $m=63$.  The $m=7$ instance
is weaker than the published numerical interval
$21\le\CAN_1(7,9,2)\le24$; the point is the uniform asymptotic separation.
\end{remark}

\section{The smallest illustration and provenance}

The established radius-covering-array tables give
$\CAN_1(4,6,2)=5$.  Theorem~\ref{thm:dictionary} therefore yields
\[
  \iota(Q_6,Q_2)=5>4=\gamma(Q_4).
\]
One minimum set is
\[
 \{000000,000011,000101,111001,111110\}.
\]
This numerical value is not claimed as new.  An independent proof, exhaustive
Python enumeration, and a Lean finite certificate are retained for direct
auditing in graph language.

\section{Formal verification boundary}

The accompanying Lean development verifies the coordinate-copy theorem, the
exact distance-to-face identity, the equivalence between face hitting and the
radius-covering-array predicate, exact-cover disjointness, projected minimum
distance, puncturing-to-full-distance lifting, and the abstract Hamming packing
step.  It also checks the algebraic components of
Theorem~\ref{thm:quantitative-gap}: the multiplicity estimate, deletion-pair
rescaling, Caro--Wei rearrangement, monotonicity, final inequality combination,
and the exact $m=7,15,31,63$ arithmetic.

Two boundaries remain explicit.  First, the Hamming-ball cardinality formulas
and the full incidence sums in Theorem~\ref{thm:quantitative-gap} have not yet
been assembled into one end-to-end Lean theorem.  Second, the finite $(6,2)$
certificate's exhaustive four-row lower bound uses \texttt{native\_decide},
with the generated axiom reported explicitly.  Neither boundary affects the
human proofs, but both must be closed before describing the complete paper as
fully formally verified.

\section{Novelty boundary and open directions}

The value $\CAN_1(4,6,2)=5$ is prior art.  The candidate-new contribution is
the graph--coding dictionary together with the perfect-projection obstruction,
the Hamming-family equality classification, and the quantitative gap theorem.
Searches through radius-covering arrays, generalized surjective codes, perfect
codes, puncturing, and robust projections have not located these statements,
but that does not establish priority.  Independent review by researchers in
covering arrays, coding theory, and domination theory is required.

The dictionary also exposes further problems: determine sharp logarithmic
constants for fixed $m,r$; improve the polynomial loss in the quantitative gap;
characterize equality outside perfect-code lengths; and extend the theory to
Hamming graphs and other Cartesian products.
