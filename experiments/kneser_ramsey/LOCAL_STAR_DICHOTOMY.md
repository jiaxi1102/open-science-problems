# Nine-point local-star dichotomy

## Statement

Let \(X\) be a set of nine points and color every triple of \(X\) red or blue.
Assume that no partition of \(X\) into three triples is monochromatic. Then at
least one of the following holds.

1. **Point type.** One color class has a common point.
2. **Barrier type.** There is a partition \(X=S\sqcup T\), with
   \(|S|=4\) and \(|T|=5\), such that every triple contained in \(S\) has
   one color and every triple contained in \(T\) has the other color.

The alternatives are not asserted to be exclusive. The point alternative is
actually rigid: if all red triples contain \(p\), then a blue triple containing
\(p\) would leave six points that can be partitioned into two triples avoiding
\(p\). Those two triples must be blue, producing a blue perfect matching.
Therefore

\[
\chi(E)=\text{red}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad p\in E,
\]

up to swapping the color names. Thus point type means the exact point-star
coloring, not merely a subfamily with a common point.

## Why this is the relevant local theorem

Fix a vertex \(A\in \binom{[12]}3\) in a hypothetical red-blue coloring of
\(KG(12,3)\) with no monochromatic triangle. Its neighbors are the 84 triples
of the nine-point complement \([12]\setminus A\). Color a neighboring triple
\(B\) by the color of the Kneser edge \(AB\).

A partition of \([12]\setminus A\) into three triples \(B,C,D\) gives a
\(K_4\) on \(A,B,C,D\). If \(AB,AC,AD\) had one color, then the three edges
among \(B,C,D\) would all have the other color; either way the \(K_4\) would
contain a monochromatic triangle. Hence the induced coloring on the nine-point
complement has no monochromatic perfect matching and satisfies the dichotomy.

By transitivity of \(S_{12}\), the stabilizer of \(A\), and global color swap,
every global solution therefore falls into one of only two canonical cases at
\(A_0=\{0,1,2\}\):

- **Canonical point case:** \(A_0B\) is red exactly when \(3\in B\). This
  fixes all 84 edges incident with \(A_0\).
- **Canonical barrier case:** every \(B\in\binom{\{3,4,5,6\}}3\) gives a red
  edge \(A_0B\), while every
  \(B\in\binom{\{7,8,9,10,11\}}3\) gives a blue edge. This fixes 14 incident
  edges.

Consequently, deciding these two cases decides whether \(KG(12,3)\) admits a
red-blue edge-coloring without a monochromatic triangle.

## Machine certificate

`tools/kneser_local_star_dichotomy.py` encodes the negation of the theorem.
There are:

- 84 Boolean variables, one for each triple of a nine-set;
- 280 partitions into three triples;
- 560 NAE clauses excluding monochromatic perfect matchings;
- 18 clauses asserting that neither color has a common point;
- 252 clauses excluding every oppositely colored \(4+5\) core;
- 830 clauses in total.

The workflow `.github/workflows/kneser-local-star-dichotomy.yml`:

1. generates the deterministic DIMACS instance and semantic clause map;
2. checks explicit sharpness examples of both structural types;
3. obtains an UNSAT proof from pinned CaDiCaL;
4. verifies that proof with pinned DRAT-trim;
5. cross-checks the result with CaDiCaL195, Glucose42, and Minisat22;
6. extracts an irreducible clause core for human-proof reconstruction.

The finite dichotomy is therefore machine certified. A concise human proof is
still being extracted from the irreducible core and should replace the SAT
certificate as the primary exposition.

## Claim boundary

This theorem does **not** by itself prove that \(KG(12,3)\) is Ramsey for a
triangle. It proves that a complete exact search needs only the canonical point
and barrier cases. Those cases are generated by
`tools/kneser_r3_local_cases.py` and searched independently.
