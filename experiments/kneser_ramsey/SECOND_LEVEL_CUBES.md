# Second-level canonical decomposition of `KG(12,3)`

## Purpose

The exact question is whether the edges of `KG(12,3)` can be colored red and
blue without a monochromatic triangle.  The certified nine-point local-star
dichotomy reduces any hypothetical coloring to one of two first-level cases at

\[
A=\{0,1,2\}.
\]

Applying the same dichotomy at a carefully chosen red neighbor

\[
D=\{3,4,5\}
\]

and quotienting by the stabilizer of the first configuration gives a finite,
complete second-level decomposition:

- 8 canonical cubes below the first-level **point** case;
- 15 canonical cubes below the first-level **barrier** case;
- 23 cubes in total.

The orbit representatives and internal audits are generated by
`tools/kneser_r3_point_cubes.py` and
`tools/kneser_r3_barrier_cubes.py`.

## Certified eliminations

CaDiCaL 1.9.5, Glucose 4.2, and MiniSat 2.2.  CaDiCaL emitted one DRAT proof
per cube, and every proof was accepted by DRAT-trim commit
`a4f58d369e4b45ab5ea4cfc82595887c63e2f950`.

## Ten unresolved cubes

The following cubes were not decided within the bounded local portfolio runs:

### First-level point branch

- `P-point-red-A`
- `P-barrier-a0`
- `P-barrier-a1-red`
- `P-barrier-a1-blue`
- `P-barrier-a2-red`

### First-level barrier branch

- `B-point-red-A`
- `B-point-blue-s`
- `B-barrier-a0-b1`
- `B-barrier-a1-b1-red`
- `B-barrier-a1-b1-blue`

The surviving barrier-branch representatives share a conspicuous structural
feature: the second local point or four-set uses the distinguished bridge point
`6`.  All tested barrier cubes avoiding that point, all `a=2` and `a=3`
barriers, and the point type centered in the five-point side are certified
UNSAT.  This is a theorem-discovery signal, not yet a proved classification of
the ten survivors.

## Current logical boundary

The thirteen certificates are complete proofs for their individual cubes.
Together with the certified local-star dichotomy and the audited orbit split,
they reduce the exact `KG(12,3)` problem to the ten cubes listed above.  They do
**not** yet prove that `KG(12,3)` is Ramsey for a triangle and do not establish

\[
R_3^{KG}(3,3)=12.
\]

A satisfying assignment in any survivor would instead certify
`R_3^{KG}(3,3)=13`, using the previously known upper bound.  The next goal is
therefore an exact decision of all ten survivors, followed by either model
validation or proof-certificate checking.
